Gogo Design System · Wave 1

Mini-map Menus

Every add-on with a mini-map button, drawn from its code: the tooltip it shows today beside the menu it becomes. Flip a scenario and both sides follow. The menus are clickable: hover a gold title with an arrow for its flyout, click Toggle to flip a feature.

How to reviewEvery menu has its mini-map button beside it, and the menu opens to its left as it does in game. Click the menu's rows the way a controller would, or click the button with Left, Right or Middle, with or without Shift, the way a mouse player would. The line under each menu says what the click did, and both ways change the same switches. Under each menu, the options panel's Features section is wired to the same switches: tick a box and the menu follows, click a row and the box follows.

Will It Fit?

Every one-line menu row from all six add-ons, in all 11 languages: 682 rows, measured the way Menu-Kit sizes them. English fits a 320-point menu everywhere; 55 rows in German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Russian don't. Chinese and Korean fit. Two rules make every one fit:

  • Short on the right. A title row's right side is a state word (On, Off, Active, Paused). A row's right side is otherwise a number, money, a ratio, a game slot name or a click. Anything longer or variable goes in a note under the description, which wraps.
  • Long on the left wraps. No row widens the menu past 320. A label too long for its row wraps onto a second line, and its right side stays level with the first.

The worst cases, in their own language. Before is today's kit, which widens the menu to fit; after is the two rules. The widths are measured in the game font's proportions; the page draws in a narrower stand-in, so a before can look roomier here than it is in game.

Components

The menu pieces every add-on builds from. After the plate and spacing come Menu-Kit's row kinds today. What's marked proposed is new: an item row, a read-out row, and the button's shortcut named on an action row.
